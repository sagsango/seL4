==============================================================================
seL4 CAPABILITY LIFECYCLE                                    AArch64 lab guide
==============================================================================

Deleting the parent's copy leaves the child's copy valid. To remove authority
from other CSpaces, revoke from a capability that the kernel recognizes as
their derivation ancestor. To stop a child process, manage its threads and
resources explicitly through the process server.

1. CAPABILITIES, SLOTS, AND PROCESSES
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  Term           Meaning
  -------------  ------------------------------------------------------------
  CPtr           A userspace value resolved through the caller's CSpace.
  CSpace         The capability namespace accessible to a thread.
  CNode          A kernel object containing capability slots.
  CTE            A kernel slot entry containing cap_t and cteMDBNode.
  cap_t          Object reference or control authority, plus type-specific
                 fields such as rights, badge, or mapping metadata.
  MDB            Kernel-maintained links and flags used to identify related
                 capabilities and determine when a capability is the last
                 one to an object.
  TCB            The kernel object representing a thread.

A process parent is a userspace relationship. An MDB parent is a relationship
between capability slots. Creating a thread through process-server does not
create a kernel rule that stops the thread when its creator stops.

2. HOW A CAPABILITY REACHES ANOTHER PROCESS
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  Operation                    Kernel action
  ---------------------------  ----------------------------------------------
  seL4_Untyped_Retype          Creates objects from untyped memory and inserts
                               their initial caps with insertNewCap().

  seL4_CNode_Copy              Resolves the source and empty destination
                               slots, masks applicable rights, calls
                               deriveCap(), and inserts with cteInsert().

  seL4_CNode_Mint              Derives and inserts a cap with applicable
                               rights and cap data, such as an endpoint
                               badge or a CNode guard.

  IPC capability transfer      With the required IPC authority and receive
                               slot, transferCaps() derives and inserts a
                               cap into the receiver's CSpace. The sender
                               normally keeps its source cap.

  seL4_CNode_Move              Moves the existing cap and its MDB entry to
                               an empty slot. The source slot becomes empty.

cteInsert() links the destination after the source in the MDB, fixes adjacent
links, and sets flags based on the capability types and derivation. The
destination has its own cap_t, while both capabilities can name the same
object. In AArch64, deriving a page-table cap requires it to be mapped.
The derived page-table cap retains the mapping metadata.

3. EXAMPLE: PARENT DELETES ITS PAGE-TABLE CAP
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

Assume P has retyped page table T from untyped U, mapped T into the child's
VSpace, and copied the mapped cap into the child's CSpace. Assume these are
the only caps to T and the VSpace and mapping stay live.

  Before Delete(P.slot_10)                 After Delete(P.slot_10)

  P.slot_10  [cap to T] --+                 P.slot_10  [empty]
                         +--> table T
  C.slot_22  [cap to T] --+                 C.slot_22  [cap to T] --> table T

  1. CNode_Delete resolves P.slot_10 and calls cteDelete().
  2. finaliseSlot() calls isFinalCapability(). It checks the MDB neighbors
     with sameObjectAs(). The child cap means P.slot_10 is non-final.
  3. The AArch64 page-table finalizer leaves the mapping installed when the
     deleted cap is non-final.
  4. emptySlot() splices the slot out of the MDB and writes a null cap into
     the slot. C.slot_22 remains valid.
  5. If C.slot_22 is later deleted as the final cap and T is still mapped,
     Arch_finaliseCap() detaches T using its recorded ASID and address.
     Translations through T are removed, so later accesses may fault.

A final cap is the last cap to the object. Being the original cap or the
cap held by the creator does not make it final.

4. HOW TO REMOVE THE COPIES WITH REVOKE
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  Operation       Target slot                   Other capability slots
  --------------  ----------------------------  -----------------------------
  Delete          Empties the selected slot.    Surviving copies stay valid.
                                                Object finalization can also
                                                clean up slots owned by a
                                                CNode or TCB.

  Revoke          Retains the selected cap.     Deletes descendants that the
                                                kernel recognizes for it.

The MDB is a doubly linked list of CTEs. mdbPrev and mdbNext are list links.
The flags mdbRevocable and mdbFirstBadged help the kernel recognize derivation
relationships. A neighboring entry alone does not imply a revocable child.

cteRevoke() repeatedly checks the next slot with isMDBParentOf(). That check
uses the ancestor's revocability flag, sameRegionAs(), and rules for the cap
type, including badge boundaries where applicable. Each matching slot is
deleted through cteDelete(). The loop has preemption points for long cleanup.

In the page-table example, before deleting P.slot_10:

  Keep U and call Revoke(U)
      |
      +-- Deletes P.slot_10 and C.slot_22, even in separate CSpaces.
      +-- Finalizes T when its last cap is removed.
      +-- Also removes caps to other objects retyped from U.
      +-- Keeps U available for retyping once the descendants are gone.

If P.slot_10 has already been deleted, Revoke(U) can still reach C.slot_22
while U remains the recognized ancestor. Revoke(P.slot_10) cannot target
the former capability after the slot has been emptied.

In the simple two-copy case, Revoke(P.slot_10) instead keeps P.slot_10 and
deletes C.slot_22. Do not assume that every later copy can revoke copies
made from it. For example, the ARM kernel marks a plain derived page-table
copy as non-revocable, so that copy does not become a new revocation root.

Use a separate untyped allocation for a group you need to reclaim together.
Revoking a shared untyped can affect unrelated services that use its objects.

5. WHAT HAPPENS WHEN A PROCESS ENDS
------------------------------------------------------------------------------

  Event                         Effect
  ----------------------------  ---------------------------------------------
  Creator thread stops          Its child threads can continue running.
                                Capability slots do not disappear merely
                                because the creator stops executing.

  Final TCB cap is deleted      The kernel suspends the thread and cleans up
                                the TCB's internal slots. A TCB does not
                                represent a complete userspace process.

  Final CNode cap is deleted    The kernel cleans up the slots inside that
                                CNode. Caps copied into other CNodes can
                                survive, depending on what else is revoked.

  Child is destroyed by         Userspace tears down the resources it owns.
  process-server                Shared resources and references outside the
                                child's CSpace have their own lifetimes.

For final CNode and TCB cleanup, the kernel uses a temporary zombie cap to
record the work left to do. finaliseSlot() and reduceZombie() drain the
internal slots, with preemption points during cleanup.

In this lab, process-server waits on the launched app's fault/completion
endpoint. It then calls sel4utils_destroy_process(). The library revokes
derived capabilities from the owned child CNode to clear self-references,
frees the owned CSpace, cleans up the thread, tears down the owned VSpace,
and frees owned objects such as the fault endpoint and VSpace root.

If the desired policy is "when parent exits, stop its children," the
process server needs to track those relationships and explicitly stop and
clean up each affected child. Revoking an individual page-table cap can
remove memory mappings, but it does not perform that process cleanup.
